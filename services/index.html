I

Commercial strategy

Where the margin comes from

Pricing, packaging and mix. The decisions that determine whether a good product becomes a good business, answered with the numbers underneath them.

  • Pricing and packaging
  • Segment, channel and mix
  • Go-to-market economics
  • Board and ExCo strategy papers
II

Unit economics

The engine, isolated

What one customer, one site, one cohort actually earns and what it costs to get. Contribution margin traced back to its drivers, so a growth plan can be argued about on evidence.

  • Contribution margin by segment
  • Acquisition cost and payback
  • Cohort, retention and expansion
  • Capacity and cost-to-serve
III

Operating models

Built to be run weekly

The internal model the business is actually steered on: driver-based, reconciling to the accounts, updated monthly without a rebuild. Three-statement where the situation calls for it.

  • Driver-based operating models
  • Three-statement builds
  • Scenario and sensitivity analysis
  • Budget, forecast and variance
IV

Investor readiness

Legible from the other side

Narrative, model, deck and data room assembled to the standard an institutional investor expects before they will spend real diligence time on you. Written by someone who has run that diligence.

  • Equity story and investor narrative
  • Deck, model and data room
  • Diligence preparation and Q&A
  • Cap table, dilution and term sheets
V

Commercial due diligence

The same rigour, pointed outward

For anyone about to buy, back or merge with a business: the target’s numbers taken apart properly. Where the earnings actually come from, which of them survive the transaction, and what the plan assumes that the market will not give it.

  • Revenue and margin quality
  • Market, demand and competitive position
  • Management case, tested
  • Post-deal value creation plan
